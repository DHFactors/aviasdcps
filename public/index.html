Understanding Safety Reporting

Aviation safety depends on open, non-punitive reporting. Every report contributes to identifying systemic risks and preventing future occurrences in line with ICAO Annex 19 Safety Management principles.

🛡️ Just Culture

Report without fear of blame. Focus is on systemic improvement, not individual punishment, except for willful negligence.

🔎 Early Detection

Near misses and minor issues often precede serious events. Reporting them enables proactive mitigation.

📈 Continuous Improvement

Aggregated, de-identified reports drive Safety Performance Indicators (SPIs) and State Safety oversight.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to common questions about safety reporting.

Reporting Basics

Safety reporting is the systematic collection of hazard, incident, and occurrence data to identify risks and improve safety performance.

A hazard is any condition or object with the potential to cause or contribute to an incident — from technical faults to human factors.

Reporting enables early detection of systemic risks, feeding SPIs and enabling preventive action before serious events occur.

A safety occurrence is any event that has or could have implications for aviation safety, including incidents, near misses, and unsafe conditions.

Submitting Reports

Anyone involved in aviation operations — crew, maintenance, ATC, ground staff, or management — can submit a report.

Report Types

Confidential reporting protects the reporter’s identity while allowing safety data to be used for analysis.

Voluntary reports capture hazards and near misses not subject to mandatory reporting, enriching safety intelligence.
